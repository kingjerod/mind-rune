# Tiles Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Several sessions are on screen at once, side by side in one row of tiles. Each tile has a header, one tile is the focused one, a session opens "to the side" from the sidebar, tiles resize and close, a session can be renamed, and the arrangement comes back at the next start.

**Architecture:** `mr-core`'s `App` stops holding one shown session and holds a row of tiles (`Tiles`): which sessions, in which order, how wide, and which is focused. Every rule that hung on "shown" now says whether it means "in a tile" or "focused". The window draws one tile per entry of that row; each tile's root element carries the session's number in its key, so everything inside a tile is found by a key path and keeps its plain key. Dragging a sidebar row is built by hand from Rune's `on_drag`, a rectangle of the tile row kept in state, and one hit test in the sidebar.

**Tech Stack:** Rust 1.99.1 (edition 2024), `rune` and `rune-kit` at the pinned revision, `serde`, `serde_json`, std threads and channels, `tempfile` for tests. No change to dependencies.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-claude-code-ui-design.md`. This plan implements section 5.2 except the Remote Control mark, the per-tile part of 5.3, the dragging of rows in 5.1, the tile arrangement of section 9, the "two tiles asked to show the same session" case of section 10 and the "three tiles with one needing the user" screen of section 11. Read these before starting, in this order: the spec, `docs/notes-for-later-plans.md` (first "What plan 4 must know"), `docs/protocol-notes.md`.

**For the controller:** Jerod's rule since 2026-11-13: every subagent (implementer, reviewer, fixer) runs on Opus 5.5 at high effort or better. Nothing less, whatever the size of the task. Two research reports made for this plan are on disk and not in git: `.superpowers/sdd/plan-4-research/rune-facts.md` (what Rune offers, with file and line) and `.superpowers/sdd/plan-4-research/window-survey.md` (every place the window assumes one session on screen, every element key, every test helper). ## Decisions in this plan that the design does not make, or makes differently

Each is the planner's call. Jerod has not seen them; they are listed in the handover for review.

1. **"Shown" becomes two things.** A session is *in a tile* or not, and one tile is *focused*. In a tile: its row is highlighted, its history is read, it is kept in the list whatever its age. Focused: it counts as seen, Escape stops its turn, a click on a sidebar row lands in it.
2. **"Finished" shows for a tile that is not focused.** When a turn ends in a tile other than the focused one, its row and its header say "Finished" until that tile is focused. The spec says "finished: the user has not looked at the session since", and on a wide screen a tile at the far side is not looked at just because it is drawn.
3. **A click shows a session in the focused tile.** A session that is already in a tile is never shown twice: its tile takes the focus instead, whatever was asked. "New session" does the same: the new session takes the focused tile's place. The spec says it "opens an empty tile"; with one tile on screen, which is the usual case, that would add a tile at every new session.
4. **Ctrl means "to the side".** Ctrl+click on a row (Cmd+click on macOS), "Open to the side" in the row's menu, and Ctrl held while choosing a folder for a new session each open a new tile right of the focused one, and that tile takes the focus. When there is no room the menu entry is disabled, and Ctrl+click does what a plain click does.
5. **Room.** A new tile needs 420 points for every tile, itself included. The new tile takes an equal share of the row and the others shrink in proportion. The 420 points hold when a tile is opened and when a divider is dragged. When the window itself is made narrower the tiles shrink in proportion and may go below 420; none is closed.
6. **Closing a tile** moves the focus to the tile on its right, else on its left. Its session keeps running. A new session with nothing sent and nothing typed is forgotten, as when it is left today.
7. **The accent outline shows only with two or more tiles.** One tile alone is always the focused one. The amber outline of a tile that needs the user shows always.
8. **What focuses a tile.** A press on its plain content or its header (the typing focus goes to its input box), a text field in it taking the keyboard focus, and any of its controls being used.
9. **Keys.** F6 and Shift+F6 move to the next and the previous tile's input box, around the ends. Ctrl+W closes the focused tile. Escape stops the focused tile's turn.
10. **Renaming.** Double-click on a tile's title, or "Rename" in the row's menu, which edits the name in the row. A session whose program is not running cannot be renamed in the store by this app, which never writes there: the app shows the new name at once, keeps it in its own saved state, and hands it to Claude Code when that session's program next starts. Until then other apps show the old name.
11. **A name the user gave is not replaced in the same run.** After a rename, a different title coming from the program (the generated one can arrive late) is ignored until the app is started again. A rename made in another app meanwhile is not seen until then.
12. **When the arrangement is saved.** When a tile opens to the side, when a tile closes, when a divider is let go, and when the window closes. A session swapped into the focused tile is saved with the next of these.
13. **Restoring** waits until every saved tile's session is known, or until the complete listing has come; sessions that are gone are left out. Nothing is restored when the app is started with a folder.
14. **Dragging a row.** Over the tile row: with room, a line shows at the nearest edge between tiles and the drop opens a tile there; without room, the tile under the pointer is outlined and the drop shows the session in it. Over a group's header or any of its rows: the drop moves the session to that group. A row whose session is already in a tile only focuses that tile when dropped on the tile row.
15. **Double-click on a divider** gives every tile the same width.
16. **The conversation width stays one number for all tiles.** Dragging its handle to the edge of the tile it is dragged in means "no limit", as the spec says for a tile.
17. **Tiles appear, go and resize at once**, with no slide or fade, as a session shown by a click does today.
18. **Arrow keys in the sidebar** keep doing what a click does without taking the keyboard focus, so they swap the focused tile's session as they go.
19. **The row's menu** reads "Move to group", "Open to the side", "Rename", in that order.
20. **A header on every tile**, also when there is only one: the folder's name, the title ("New session" until there is one), the state in a word ("Working", "Needs you", "Finished", or nothing), and the close button.
21. **A long message of the user's is cut** like long tool output: after 20 lines or 4,000 characters, with "Show all" under it.
22. **A tile is moved by its header** (added with Task 15). Dragging a tile's header along the row shows the same line a dragged row shows, at the edge the tile would go to, and the drop puts it there with its width; nothing shows at its own two edges. Ctrl+Shift+PageUp and Ctrl+Shift+PageDown move the focused tile one place left or right, the keys a browser and a terminal move a tab with, so that it can be done without the pointer. A sidebar row whose session is already in a tile still only focuses its tile (decision 14 stands). The spec lists "Reordering tiles and groups by dragging" under later; groups stay there.

## Rune facts for the window tasks

Read from Rune's source at the pinned revision on 2026-11-14. The full report, with a file and line for every statement, is `.superpowers/sdd/plan-4-research/rune-facts.md`. Check each against the source before relying on it.

- **Layout.** Grow and shrink both default to 0, unlike CSS. `.grow(g)` means basis 0 and shrink 1, so a row of children with `.grow(share)` and `.min_w(0.0)` splits the row in proportion to the shares. A `.w(points)` child never shrinks. There is no splitter for more than two panes in the kit.
- **Rectangles.** In a view, `cx.layout_with(key_or_path, |rect| ..)` gives an element's last layout and runs the view again when it changes. `El::on_layout(|state, [x, y, w, h]| ..)` reports a rectangle into state in window points. No `Tx` call reads a rectangle by key.
- **Dragging.** `El::on_drag(|state, drag, tx| ..)` gets `Phase::Down` on every press, with no threshold, then `Move` and `Up`; `drag.dx`, `drag.dy` and `drag.window_point()` are in window points; `drag.cancelled()` is true on an `Up` caused by Escape or a lost pointer; `drag.data` is one `f32` kept between phases. Moves commit at once; `Down` and `Up` animate unless the handler calls `tx.instant()`. When the release is inside the pressed element its `on_press` runs after the `Up`, moved or not: the kit's tab strip sets a flag in `Up` that its `on_press` then swallows. No `Up` comes at all when the pressed element is gone at release (a virtual row scrolled out) or a modal opened mid-press.
- **What is under the pointer.** `tx.element_at_in(scope, x, y, |state, picked, tx| ..)` runs after the handler; `picked.path()` holds the keys of the element's ancestors, and other subtrees (a preview in the overlay layer) do not hide it. Hover styles light only the pressed element while a press is held, so a drop target's highlight comes from state. `El::on_pointer_move` is heard by every element under a held pointer.
- **Overlays.** `rune_kit::root(base, layer)` draws `layer` over the window. An element there is placed with `.absolute(x, y)`; `.inert()` lets input pass through it.
- **Presses.** A press goes to the one topmost element that has a press handler: it does not also reach a container. `tx.mods()` gives the modifiers (`cmd` is Ctrl off macOS). `El::on_double_press` pairs two presses on the same element path. A press on plain content clears the keyboard focus unless an ancestor says `preserve_focus_on_press(true)`.
- **Focus.** There is no focus callback. Rune writes the real focus into every field's `TextEdit::focused` on each change, so which tile's box has the focus is read from `SessionView::draft.focused`. `El::on_key` on a container does not hear keys while a text field inside it has the focus: window shortcuts go in `view::on_key`. `sidebar::rename_watch` shows the house way to react to a focus change: an element pushed into the layer only while a condition holds, whose `on_layout` runs the reaction.
- **Keys.** `tx.focus`, an overlay's anchor (`Select`, `Menu`, `Popover` anchor to `{key}-trigger`), `focus_return` and `rt.presented` take an exact key and use the first match in the window. `cx.layout_with`, every scroll request including `tx.keep_on_screen`, `tx.element_at_in` and `rt.find` (when the text has a `/`) accept a key path such as `mr-tile-7/mr-column`. Two siblings with the same key are an error the snapshot test fails on.
- **Borders.** `.border(width, colour)` takes no layout room. `.cursor(Cursor::ResizeColumn)` sets the pointer. A hit area needs an opacity above 0.001; the kit's grip uses a transparent fill at 0.02.
- **Tests.** Rune's `Runtime` has `pointer_move`, `pointer_down`, `pointer_up`, `key`, `wheel`, `secondary_down`, `secondary_up` and `physical_input(PhysicalInput::Modifiers(mods))`; clicks and typing are composed in `crates/app/src/testkit.rs`. A removed element stays in `rt.elements()` with `exiting: true` while it fades.

## Tasks

### Task 1: The row of tiles and its arithmetic

**Files:**
- Create: `crates/core/src/tiles.rs`
- Modify: `crates/core/src/lib.rs` (the module, and `pub use` of its public names)

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `SessionKey` from `sessions.rs`.
- Produces:
  ```rust
  /// The least a tile is opened or dragged to, in points.
  pub const MIN_TILE_WIDTH: f32 = 420.0;

  #[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
  pub struct Tile { pub key: SessionKey, /** Its part of the row against the others'; above 0. */ pub share: f32 }

  /// Where a session is shown.
  #[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
  pub enum Place {
      /// In the focused tile, in place of its session; in a first tile when there is none.
      Focused,
      /// In a new tile right of the focused one. As `Focused` when there is no room.
      Side,
      /// In a new tile at this position, 0 being leftmost. Nothing when there is no room.
      At(usize),
      /// In place of this tile's session.
      Tile(SessionKey),
  }

  /// What `Tiles::put` did.
  #[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
  pub(crate) enum Put { Nothing, FocusedOnly, Added, Replaced(SessionKey) }

  #[derive(Clone, Debug, Default, PartialEq)]
  pub struct Tiles { /* list, focused, version */ }
  impl Tiles {
      pub fn list(&self) -> &[Tile];
      pub fn focused(&self) -> Option<SessionKey>;
      pub fn contains(&self, key: SessionKey) -> bool;
      pub fn index_of(&self, key: SessionKey) -> Option<usize>;
      /// Goes up with every change, the focus included.
      pub fn version(&self) -> u64;
      /// Each tile's width in a row `row_width` wide, in order; they add up to `row_width`.
      pub fn widths(&self, row_width: f32) -> Vec<f32>;
      /// Every tile and one more could each have `MIN_TILE_WIDTH`. True for an empty row.
      pub fn fits_another(&self, row_width: f32) -> bool;
      /// The tile under `x`, measured from the row's left edge.
      pub fn tile_at(&self, row_width: f32, x: f32) -> Option<usize>;
      /// The nearest of the edges a new tile can open at: 0 to `len`.
      pub fn edge_at(&self, row_width: f32, x: f32) -> usize;
      /// `row_width` is the row's width when the window has told it.
      pub(crate) fn put(&mut self, key: SessionKey, place: Place, row_width: Option<f32>) -> Put;
      pub(crate) fn focus(&mut self, key: SessionKey) -> bool;
      /// Takes `key`'s tile out; the focus goes to the tile on its right, else on its left.
      pub(crate) fn remove(&mut self, key: SessionKey) -> bool;
      /// Makes `left`'s tile `width` wide and gives or takes the difference from the tile on
      /// its right. Neither goes below `MIN_TILE_WIDTH`; nothing happens when the two have less
      /// than twice that between them, or `left` is the last tile.
      pub(crate) fn resize(&mut self, left: SessionKey, width: f32, row_width: f32) -> bool;
      pub(crate) fn even(&mut self) -> bool;
      /// From what was saved: shares that are not finite or not above 0 become 1, a key named
      /// twice is kept once, and the focus is `focused` when it is among them, else the first.
      pub(crate) fn restored(saved: Vec<Tile>, focused: Option<SessionKey>) -> Tiles;
  }
  ```

Rules:

- `put` with a key already in the row focuses its tile and returns `FocusedOnly`, whatever the place.
- There is room for a new tile when the row's width is not known, or `fits_another(row_width)`. Without room `Side` behaves as `Focused` and `At` returns `Nothing`.
- A tile added by `Side` or `At` gets the mean of the others' shares, so it takes an equal part of the row. Then, when the row's width is known, no tile is left under `MIN_TILE_WIDTH`: a tile under it is raised to it and the difference is taken from the tiles above it, in proportion to what each has above it.
- A tile added or replaced takes the focus.
- `Tile(key)` naming a tile that is not there returns `Nothing`.
- Removing a tile leaves the others' shares as they are: they grow in proportion.

- [ ] **Step 1: Write the failing tests** in `tiles.rs`.

  - `a_session_is_never_in_two_tiles`: with `[a, b]` and `a` focused, `put(b, Side, None)`, `put(b, At(0), None)` and `put(b, Tile(a), None)` each give `FocusedOnly`, the list stays `[a, b]`, the focus is `b`.

- **The handle.** A tile's header drags its tile: a press anywhere in the header but on the close button, moved more than `drag::SLOP`. Before that nothing shows, and the release is the click it is today. The header keeps everything it has: a press focuses the tile, a double press on the title edits the name, the close button closes. A header whose title is being edited is not dragged: a press in the name field edits the name. With one tile in the row no drag starts.
- **One drag at a time, guarded as a row's.** The drag sits in `Ui.drag` with `from: DragFrom::Header`. Everything that ends or guards a row's drag does the same for it, through the same code where it can: the watcher for a release that never comes, Escape (ruling R8: it cancels the drag and does nothing else), the window's keys and the sidebar rows' keys waiting, a menu opening, `tidy_sidebar`. A header's drag also ends when its session's tile is gone.
- **The target.** Inside `Ui.row_rect`: `DropTarget::Edge(edge_at(x))` when `moved_index` says the tile would move there, else nothing. Room for another tile plays no part: no tile is added, so a row that is full still takes a move. Outside the tile row: nothing. A header's drag never asks the sidebar what is under the pointer and never has a `Group` or a `Tile` target: a tile dropped on the sidebar does nothing.
- **What shows.** The session's title in the chip by the pointer and the accent line at the target edge, as for a row. Ruling R11 holds as built: while the target is an edge, the focused tile's accent outline is not drawn, and amber stays.
- **The release.** Not cancelled and over an edge: `glue::move_tile(ui, tx, key, edge, true)`: the tile moves at once (`tx.instant()`), takes the focus, and its input box takes the keyboard. Over nothing: the drag is cleared and nothing else happens. Cancelled: the drag is cleared and nothing else happens. A header's drag never sets `Ui.swallow_row_press`: the press Rune may run after a release inside the header only focuses the tile, as a click does, and the next click on a sidebar row must work.
- **Nothing in the tile is lost by a move.** The tiles are keyed, so Rune moves them: the conversation is not rebuilt, its scroll position stays, a draft typed in the input box stays, and a request waiting in the tile still answers in its own session.
- **The keys.** Ctrl+Shift+PageUp moves the focused tile one place left, Ctrl+Shift+PageDown one place right (with the modifier the app's other keys use on macOS, as `is_close_key` does it). At the end of the row nothing moves; the row does not wrap. A held key moves on. The keyboard focus stays in the field it is in, and what was typed there stays. They wait as F6 waits: only on `Screen::Main`, not while a picker's list or a menu is open (`list_open`), not while a row or a header is dragged (`drag_key` takes them). On `Screen::Main` the key is taken (`on_key` returns true) also when nothing moved. A name being edited in a header is neither kept nor dropped by a move: it goes on being edited.
- **What stays as decided.** A sidebar row whose session is already in a tile, dropped on the tile row, only focuses its tile (decision 14).

What this task is most likely to get wrong, so the tests below pin it: Rune giving the drag to the header while the title and the close button inside it have press handlers of their own (read `on_drag` in Rune's source before choosing where the handler goes; if it must go on more than one element, one function serves them all); a reorder of keyed children dropping the keyboard focus or the draft of a field inside (see `glue::let_go` for what happens to a field that unmounts while focused); the key reaching `view::on_key` while the keyboard is in an input box.
